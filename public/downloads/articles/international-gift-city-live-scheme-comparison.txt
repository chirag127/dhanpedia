THE TAX-TREATMENT RESOLUTION (CLOSING THE WAVE 22 CONFLICT)
===========================================================

The two 2026 secondary guides genuinely disagreed: one (Deepak Wealth) described GIFT City gains as a foreign asset on the Indian side (12.5% no-indexation LTCG + Schedule FA), while DSP's own hub described the position as fund-level — "tax is paid at the fund level using the fund's PAN, not against the investor's PAN; investors pay no capital-gains tax on redemption in India, and no TDS is deducted on redemptions or distributions." The DSP position is the primary anchor here. It also states the reported fund-level schedule: 14.95% LTCG (>24 months), 42.74% STCG, and adds the operational corollary that no Schedule FA is required on the DSP position.

The honest resolution, in the order a planner should apply it:
1. Read the scheme's offer document. If the scheme explicitly carries the DSP fund-level position, use the DSP numbers (14.95% / 42.74%, no Schedule FA, no TDS on redemption).
2. If the scheme is taxed on the Indian-side foreign-asset rules (a subset of schemes, often the more inbound or domestic-equity-flavoured GIFT City structures), use the 12.5% no-indexation LTCG + Schedule FA.
3. A CA can confirm the specific scheme's posture — this is a 30-minute call, not a structural unknown, and the difference on a Rs. 5L sleeve over 5 years is a few lakhs, not a guess.

WHY THE TER GAP MATTERS (THE COST-DECAY LAYER)
==============================================

The all-in TER spread between the routes is the quiet, compounding cost difference:
• PPFAS passive FoFs: ~0.35–0.60% all-in (the cheapest GIFT City options; the scheme TER is 0.30% plus the underlying-ETF TER).
• DSP Global Equity: up to ~1.75% — a 4–5× cost gap vs the passive FoFs, and the price of active global management.
• Inbound GIFT City domestic-equity funds (Tata): ~2.5% — the most expensive of the group.
• Direct LRS low-cost US ETF (VOO-class): ~0.03% TER — essentially the floor of the global-investing cost curve.

At 0.32 pp/year between a direct VOO-class ETF and the PPFAS S&P 500 FoF all-in TER, a Rs. 5L sleeve compounded over 20 years loses roughly Rs. 65k–Rs. 90k to the TER gap alone (the gift-city-route-comparison calculator computes this for your exact numbers). The question is whether that cost buys you the operational simplicity of the GIFT City route.

WHICH ROUTE ACTUALLY WINS
=========================

The two-route decision is not a fixed ranking; it depends on three things:
1. The CG-rate spread. The GIFT City route's embedded fund-level CG tax (14.95% on LTCG per DSP's schedule) is higher than the direct route's 12.5%. So on the tax rate alone, the direct route wins. The 2.45 pp spread on a Rs. 5L sleeve that gains Rs. 2L over 5 years is ~Rs. 49k more in tax on the GIFT City side.
2. The TER gap. The direct route wins by ~0.32 pp/year in the PPFAS vs VOO comparison, and by more if you choose the DSP active fund (0.35% vs 1.75% = 1.4 pp/year gap).
3. The Schedule FA + operational-cost layer. The GIFT City route (DSP position) avoids Schedule FA disclosure and the USD-brokerage/repatriation mechanics; the direct route carries both. For a small sleeve, the GIFT City operational edge can exactly offset the combined 2.45 pp CG-rate + 0.32 pp TER economic disadvantage.

The worked example below shows the arithmetic for a Rs. 5L sleeve over 5 years at a 10% underlying return.

WORKED EXAMPLE: RS. 5L SLEEVE, 10% INDEX RETURN, 5 YEARS
========================================================

Assume: Rs. 5,00,000 deployed, 10% gross index return p.a., 5 years, PPFAS S&P 500 FoF all-in TER 0.35%, DSP fund-level 14.95% LTCG, no Schedule FA; vs a direct VOO-class ETF at 0.03% TER, 12.5% LTCG, Schedule FA.

: Pre-tax FV (TER netted in),₹5L × (1.0965)⁵ ≈ **₹7.99L**,₹5L × (1.0997)⁵ ≈ **₹8.06L**  |  GIFT City (PPFAS S&P 500 FoF): Realized gain,≈ ₹2.99L,≈ ₹3.06L  |  Direct LRS (VOO-class): CG on the gain,14.95% × ₹2.99L ≈ **₹446k**,12.5% × ₹3.06L ≈ **₹383k**

The direct route wins by ~Rs. 13k on the corpus (0.26 pp net over 5 years). The GIFT City route wins on operational simplicity (no Schedule FA, no USD brokerage, no repatriation) — which, at Rs. 5L, is worth at least the ~Rs. 13k gap if you value your time, and more if the Schedule FA compliance cost is a real burden. At Rs. 25L+ (the PMS/PRIM rung in the capital-ladder article (./pf-capital-ladder-10l-to-1cr.mdx)), the economic gap grows faster than the operational burden, and the direct route's TER + CG advantage starts to clearly outweigh the GIFT City simplicity premium.

The honest verdict on this specific example: the direct LRS route wins on money, the GIFT City route wins on operational simplicity. A Rs. 5L sleeve is close to the crossover point; a Rs. 25L+ sleeve is clearly on the direct side.

THE "WHEN GIFT CITY IS THE RIGHT TOOL" LAYER
============================================

GIFT City (the DSP position) is the right tool when:
• You want the simplest operational path to global exposure — one INR→USD remittance through LRS, no Schedule FA, no USD brokerage, no repatriation mechanics.
• Your sleeve is Rs. 5L–Rs. 25L — the operational-cost premium is still inside the range where the TER + CG-rate gap is not yet large enough to dominate.
• The specific scheme is a passive index FoF (PPFAS S&P 500 / Nasdaq 100) — the cost is already at the floor of the GIFT City tier (~0.35% all-in), so you are not paying the 1.75% DSP active-fund premium.

GIFT City is the wrong tool when:
• You choose the DSP Global Equity active fund for a global-sleeve purpose — the ~1.75% TER is 4× the passive FoF and you are buying the active edge, not the route. At that cost, a direct low-cost US index ETF + a separate active-sleeve is cheaper.
• Your sleeve is Rs. 25L+ — the direct route's TER + 12.5% CG advantage compounds faster than the GIFT City simplicity premium.
• The specific scheme is taxed on the Indian-side foreign-asset rules (not the DSP fund-level position) — you inherit the Schedule FA + 12.5% no-indexation CG and lose the fund-level simplicity edge. Read the offer document first.

THE ONE-LINE VERDICT
====================

The 2026 GIFT City live list is real: PPFAS S&P 500 FoF and PPFAS Nasdaq 100 FoF (passive, ~0.35–0.60% all-in), DSP Global Equity Fund (active, up to ~1.75%), and the newly-launched Edelweiss Greater China and Marcellus Global Equities (actively managed), with the Tata India Dynamic Equity fund a separate inbound route at a USD 500 ticket. The tax treatment is resolved on the DSP primary anchor: fund-level (14.95% LTCG / 42.74% STCG, no investor-side CG, no TDS, no Schedule FA) — but confirm the specific scheme's offer document before remitting. On the money, the direct LRS route (0.03% VOO-class + 12.5% + Schedule FA) wins by a small margin on a Rs. 5L sleeve over 5 years (~0.26 pp net); on the operational, the GIFT City route wins by a wide margin. The crossover point, where the economic edge clearly outweighs the simplicity premium, is roughly Rs. 25L+ — the same number as the PMS/PRIM rung in the capital-ladder article, which is not a coincidence: it is the point at which the fixed operational costs of the direct route start to amortize into a genuinely different tier of investor.

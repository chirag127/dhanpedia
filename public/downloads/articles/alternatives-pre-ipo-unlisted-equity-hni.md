
Your capital ladder so far: a SIF at ₹10L, a PMS at ₹50L, an AIF at ₹1Cr. This article is the rung
most investors try to skip **directly** — buying **pre-IPO / unlisted equity in your own demat,
off-market, before the IPO** — and the three structural facts that make it both the most
misunderstood and the most expensive to get wrong.

## The three facts that define this market

**1. You cannot sell into the listing pop (ICDR Regulation 17).** Pre-IPO shares held by anyone
other than the promoters are **locked in for six months from the date of IPO allotment**, under
Regulation 17 of the SEBI ICDR Regulations. That single rule means: the listing-day price is *not
a price you can sell into*. You carry **six months of post-listing volatility with no ability to
exit** — recent listings have moved 30-40% inside that window, in both directions. The correct
question is therefore not *"what will it pop to on listing day?"* but *"at what price does it trade
**six months after** listing, and is my entry below *that*?"*

A worked case from 2026 makes the point concrete: unlisted NSE buyers paid roughly ₹1,950-2,050
per share, the September 2026 IPO priced at ₹1,785 through institutional book-building, the stock
listed at ₹1,800 and dipped toward ₹1,761 within days. Pre-IPO holders sat in the six-month
lock-in and could not sell; IPO allottees could. **The entry was above the IPO price before the
lock-in even ended.**

**2. The off-market platforms are not SEBI-recognised.** SEBI's advisories (August 2016, December
2024, June 2026) are consistent: platforms facilitating trades in unlisted securities are **not
authorised or recognised by SEBI**, so a trade done through them has **no clearing guarantee and no
SEBI-administered investor protection**. A private unlisted transaction is legal when it complies
with company law and the company's articles — but any platform claiming SEBI approval for unlisted
share trading should be treated as a red flag. This is the counterparty risk that a listed order
book does not carry.

**3. There is no single price — only a dealer's ask.** No exchange, no order book, no published
volume. What you are quoted is a dealer's current ask, which moves on **inventory** (a dealer with
a large position prices to move it), **lot size** (a 100-share lot is not a 5,000-share lot),
**IPO news flow** (a DRHP filing or a credible listing date moves quotes within hours), and the
**last funding round** (a new round re-anchors the reference valuation). The right question to a
counterparty is not *"what is the price?"* but *"at that price, what size can you actually settle,
and by when?"* — the gap between the quote and the fill is where this market hides its cost.

## Where the supply comes from (and what each source implies)

| Source | Seller motivation | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| **Employee ESOPs** | Vested, tax due on exercise, want to realise | Most common source; volume spikes after vesting cliffs |
| **Early angels / seed** | Fund life ending or rebalancing after a mark-up | Usually large, clean lots |
| **PE / VC secondary** | Fund winding down, LP redemptions | Often carries a negotiated floor price |
| **Promoter sell-down** | Pre-IPO dilution planning, personal liquidity | Worth asking *why*, and how much |
| **Pre-IPO placement** | A formal round shortly before the IPO | Priced off the expected IPO band; rarely in retail size |

None of these sellers is obliged to transact. When a name is in demand and supply is thin, the
quoted price moves on *availability*, not on anything that happened inside the business.

## The tax split that decides what you keep

This is the gap most pages selling pre-IPO shares never state cleanly. Unlisted and pre-IPO equity
is taxed **differently from listed equity**, and the two holding tests do not line up:

| | Listed equity (STT paid) | Unlisted / pre-IPO |
|---|---|---|
| Long-term holding | **12 months** | **24 months** |
| LTCG rate | 12.5% (112A), above the **shared ₹1.25L pool** | **12.5% (112), no indexation, NO pool** |
| Short-term rate | 20% (111A) | **Your slab rate** |
| STT on the transfer | Yes | No (off-market) |

Three consequences that actually change your plan:

- **An unlisted long-term gain (held 24 months+) is 12.5% with no ₹1.25L exemption.** The pool that
  shelters your listed gains does not apply here. The [after-tax wedge article](./alternatives-pms-sif-aif-after-tax-wedge.mdx)
  uses the same 12.5% number, but the *pool* is what separates the two.
- **An unlisted short-term gain (held 24 months or less) is at your slab**, not a flat 20%. For a
  top-slab investor that is a materially different (worse) outcome — which is why a short holding
  period is rarely the right plan for a pre-IPO position.
- **Sequence matters.** The holding clock starts at your acquisition *transfer*, not at listing.
  Hold unlisted for 18 months, then sell four months *after* listing on the exchange: you are past
  24 months in total, but the sale is now of a **listed** share, so the 12-month 112A test applies.
  Take advice on your specific sequence.

The [pre-IPO tax calculator](/calculators/pre-ipo-tax) runs all four heads (unlisted-LTCG,
unlisted-STCG-at-slab, listed-LTCG-above-pool, listed-STCG-20%) for your numbers.

## The decision framework, and when to say no

| Question | If the answer is bad |
|---|---|
| Is my entry below the price **six months after** listing, not the listing pop? | The ICDR Reg 17 lock-in is your real cost — re-price the entry |
| Do I have a fillable size at that price, settled by a date I can meet? | A quote you cannot settle is a dealer's ask, not a price |
| Is the counterparty transparent, and what is last comparable trade settled at? | Opaque discovery + no clearing guarantee = a premium for risk you are carrying for free |
| If the IPO is delayed or prices below my entry, can I hold for a decade? | A company with no credible listing plan is unlisted equity, not a pre-IPO bet — only exit is a private buyer at an agreed price |
| At my slab, is the short-term treatment (if I exit before 24 months) acceptable? | For a 30% slab investor, 24-month short-term at slab is rarely the plan; hold through or exit cleanly |

**When to say no:** the IPO may not happen (DRHPs lapse and get withdrawn), the IPO may price below
your entry (public-market comparables have been the more conservative number more than once), the
platform is unrecognised and off-market, and the "price" is a thin dealer ask with no comparable
fills. Two of those four at the same time is enough.

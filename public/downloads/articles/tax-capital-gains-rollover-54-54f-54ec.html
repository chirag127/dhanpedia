Capital Gains Rollover: Section 54, 54F & 54EC — Reinvest the Gain, Not the Sale Value (and What Breaks It)

The reinvestment layer over capital gains: how Section 54 (house to house), 54F (any asset to house) and 54EC (notified bonds, ₹50L cap) shelter a long-term gain, the time windows that make or break each, the proportional rule when you reinvest less than the gain, the 12.5% no-indexation base they all sit on, and why only a long-term gain qualifies.

DhanPedia · Author Chirag Singhal · Verified 2026-10-18

The loss-harvesting article covers offsetting gains with losses. This is the other side of the same ledger: when you have a long-term gain and no loss to absorb it, you can often shelter it by reinvesting it — not by the raw sale value, but by the gain. After the Finance (No.2) Act 2024 rationalisation (LTCG at 12.5%, no indexation, two holding periods of 1 and 2 years), the reinvestment exemptions under Sections 54, 54F and 54EC are the primary legal mechanism to reduce or eliminate that tax — and they work for residents and NRIs alike.

Two things decide whether you use 54, 54F or 54EC: what you sold (house → 54/54EC; any other long-term asset → 54F) and whether you want another house or liquidity. 54EC is the one that keeps you out of the housing market: a 5-year bond, fully listed, that shelters up to ₹50L of the gain.

<2>The proportional rule: it is the gain, not the sale value

This is the single most-misunderstood point, and it is why "I sold for ₹1 Cr, so I reinvest ₹1 Cr" is the wrong mental model.

$$ \text{Exemption} \;=\; \min\big(\text{net capital GAIN},\; \text{amount REINVESTED within the window}\big) $$

You shelter the gain, and only up to what you actually reinvest. Worked: a ₹8L gain from a house, reinvest ₹5L in 54EC bonds → ₹5L exempt, ₹3L still taxed at 12.5% (₹37,500). If instead you reinvest ₹60L of a ₹6L gain through 54EC, the exemption is capped at the ₹50L bond limit, so ₹10L of the gain is still taxable. The capital-gains-rollover calculator runs this split for you across all three routes.

<2>The two things that break the exemption

A rollover that quietly fails is worse than no plan at all, because you have already locked the money away. Two conditions zero the exemption:

  1. The time window is missed. 54EC needs the bonds bought within 6 months of the sale; 54

needs the new house purchased within 1 year before / 2 years after (or constructed within 3 years); 54F needs the house within 2 years. If you cannot complete it in time, the exemption fails for the uninvested amount.

  1. The gain is not a long-term gain. 54/54F/54EC apply only to long-term capital gains.

Property must have been held 24 months; listed equity / equity MFs 12 months. A short-term gain gets no rollover exemption at all.

<3>The escape valve: the Capital Gains Account Scheme (CGAS)

If you have sold but cannot complete the reinvestment inside the window, you can park the unspent consideration in a CGAS account (a designated savings/FD). The exemption survives only if that money is deployed within the remaining time; once the window closes, the exempt portion is recomputed down to what was actually invested and the shortfall becomes taxable in the year the deadline lapses. Track the CGAS deadline as a hard date.

<2>The 12.5% base they all sit on (and the cap worth knowing)

These exemptions work on top of the post-2024 rate structure: LTCG at 12.5% without indexation, holding periods of 1 year (listed) and 2 years (everything else). Two further points from that same rationalisation matter when you plan a rollover:

  • The ₹10 Cr cap (from AY 2024-25): the 54/54F exemption for a single individual/HUF is now

limited to a new-asset cost of ₹10 Cr — irrelevant to a 10L ladder, but it binds a multi-asset sale.

  • NRIs are not shut out. An NRI selling Indian property faces the same 12.5% LTCG — and can use

the same 54/54EC/54F routes, which also cut the 195 TDS the buyer is withholding. See the NRI tax article for the TDS side.

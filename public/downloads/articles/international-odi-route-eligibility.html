ODI Route Eligibility: Investing INTO a Foreign Entity (400% Net Worth Entity / LRS USD 250k Individual, FY 2026-27)

The outbound-investment layer the personal LRS / GIFT City / FAST-DS articles do not cover: investing INTO a foreign entity (an overseas start-up, a foreign JV) is ODI - a different route from buying a GIFT City fund or remitting under LRS. For an INDIAN ENTITY the automatic route is 400% of net worth (the 26 May 2026 amendment raised it from 300%), with the USD 1B FY financial-commitment trigger moving to a prior-approval route; for a RESIDENT INDIVIDUAL the ODI is equity-only and capped at the LRS USD 250,000/FY. Shows whether your stake is ODI at all (unlisted, listed >=10%, or any control stake) versus OPI, whether it fits the automatic route, and the Form FC-ODI filing.

DhanPedia · Author Chirag Singhal · Verified 2026-10-18

The GIFT City / direct-LRS articles cover buying global exposure (a GIFT City fund, a US ETF). This page covers the distinct outbound-investment question: putting money into a foreign entity - an overseas start-up, a foreign JV, a subsidiary you set up abroad. That is ODI (Overseas Direct Investment), a different route from both buying a GIFT City fund and from a personal LRS remittance, and it has its own limits, its own definition, and its own filing. Everything below is a route-eligibility model, not a Form FC-ODI filing; the operative multiple is a 2026 position that a later notification can move.

<2>ODI is not OPI and not the LRS (the definition that decides everything)

The first thing that confuses people is that "investing abroad" is not one route - it is at least three, and the definition of ODI is what puts your specific investment in the right bucket:

RouteWhat it isThe limitWho
ODI (this page),Investing into a foreign entity: unlisted equity capital, OR a listed stake of ≥10%, OR any stake that carries control (even under 10%),Entity: 400% of net worth (2026) / Individual: LRS USD 250k (equity-only),Indian entity OR resident individualOPI,Buying listed foreign securities under 10%, no control (a portfolio position),50% of net worth (entity),Entity (individuals use LRS, not OPI)LRS personal remittance,Remitting your own money (a GIFT City fund, a US brokerage, tuition, travel),USD 250,000 / FY (resident; NRIs excluded),Resident individual

So the three lines that decide ODI are: unlisted equity, a listed stake of 10% or more, or a control stake of any size. A listed foreign stake under 10% with no control is OPI, not ODI - it runs on the 50%-of-net-worth route, and this article's calculator deliberately excludes it ("Is this ODI at all? → OPI"). That boundary is the most common mis-classification: people call a 5% listed position an ODI when it is an OPI, and then file the wrong form under the wrong limit.

<2>The two investor types (entity 400% vs individual LRS - and why that gap matters)

The 2026 limits diverge sharply by who is investing:

  • An Indian entity (company / LLP / partnership) may invest up to 400% of its net worth (last audited) on the automatic route - the 26 May 2026 amendment raised the omnibus-route limit from 300% to 400%. Above a USD 1B-per-FY financial commitment, the investment moves to a prior-RBI-approval route (not just a post-fact filing).
  • A resident individual may make an ODI only in equity capital (no loans, no guarantees to the foreign entity), only within the LRS USD 250,000/FY limit (combined with all other LRS remittances), through the AD bank.

That gap is the whole strategic point: an individual's ODI ceiling is ~USD 250k, but the same investment by the individual's company is 400% of that company's net worth - which, for a company with a modest ₹20 cr net worth, is roughly USD 9.5M. So "can I invest in this foreign start-up?" has a radically different answer depending on whether the money leaves your bank account (LRS, USD 250k) or a company you own (ODI, 400% of net worth). The odi-route-eligibility calculator prices both and flags which one you are in.

The entity route is also the answer to the "good returns that need more capital" question the user keeps asking: an ODI into a foreign equity/VC is precisely a higher-capital, higher-return route - the 400%-of-net-worth limit is what lets a company deploy far more than any individual can under LRS. It pairs with the pre-IPO / unlisted HNI article (the entity is the vehicle for a pre-IPO foreign round) and the capital-inflow-migration planner (the ODI is one of the inflow/exit events that resizes the corpus).

<2>The Form FC-ODI layer (and why "a bank transfer" is not "ODI")

ODI is not a simple outward remittance - it is a regulated filing chain that begins before the money leaves:

  • The form: Form FC-ODI (entity) - filed with the AD bank, post-fact, reporting the investment. A late filing attracts a late-submission fee (about ₹7,500 and rising, scaled to the delay). [src-odi-rules-2026]
  • Individuals remit under LRS (the USD 250k cap), with Form 15CA/15CB where the remittance crosses the value threshold; there is no Form FC-ODI for a personal ODI - the LRS is the route.
  • The money-movement is a FEMA fact, independent of the tax side. A lawful ODI is clean on both ledgers; a breach of the ODI limit is a FEMA contravention priced by the FEMA 3x / compounding layer - which is why the ODI article and the FEMA article are two sides of the same coin.

The practical rule the foreign-exposure literature flags: "ODI is just a bank transfer" is the exact wrong mental model. It is a filing that precedes the remittance, and skipping it turns a lawful ODI into a Section 13 contravention.

<2>When ODI is the right tool (the honest layer)
  • Right when you are putting equity into a foreign entity (an overseas start-up, a JV you control, a foreign sub) and the investor is the entity - that is the 400% route, and it is the only way to deploy the kind of "good-return, more-capital" money the user is asking about, because the individual LRS ceiling (USD 250k) is too small for a real ODI ticket.
  • Right for an individual only when the ticket is small (under ~USD 250k) and it is equity-only - a personal ODI above that, or one that includes a loan/guarantee to the foreign entity, is simply not permitted for an individual.
  • Wrong when the "investment" is a listed foreign stake under 10% with no control - that is OPI, a different route (the 50%-of-net-worth limit), and filing it as ODI is a mis-classification.
  • Wrong when the commitment is above USD 1B/FY for an entity without a prior-RBI-approval plan - that is the approval route, not the automatic one.
<2>A worked example: a ₹20 cr company investing USD 2M in a foreign start-up

Assume an Indian company with a ₹20 cr (₹200,000,000) net worth (last audited, at 84 INR/USD) wants to take a 15% unlisted stake in a foreign start-up for USD 2,000,000.

Contrast the same USD 2M ticket from the individual personally (LRS, equity-only): the limit is USD 250k/FY, so a USD 2M personal ODI is out of route - it must come from the entity. That single reclassification - personal → entity - is the difference between a "not permitted" ODI and a clean automatic-route ODI, and it is the lever that makes ODI the "good-return, more-capital" route the user asked about. <2>What to do now (the ODI order)
  1. Classify first: ODI or OPI? - unlisted / 10% or more listed / control → ODI; a listed position under 10% with no control → OPI (a different limit and form). This is the gate the calculator's "Is this ODI at all?" row surfaces.
  2. Pick the investor: entity or individual. - The entity gets the 400% route; the individual is capped at LRS USD 250k and equity-only. The reclassification is often the whole answer.
  3. Check the commitment against the limit (400% of net worth for an entity; USD 250k for an individual) and against the USD 1B FY approval trigger (entity only).
  4. Run the odi-route-eligibility calculator for the ODI-vs-OPI verdict, the automatic-route fit, and the approval-route flag.
  5. File before remitting - Form FC-ODI (entity) / LRS + 15CA/15CB (individual); a skipped filing turns a lawful ODI into a FEMA Section 13 exposure (the FEMA layer).
  6. Reconcile with a FEMA practitioner - the 400% multiple is the 26 May 2026 position and the FC-ODI fees are case-specific; confirm the operative figures for your filing date.
<2>The one-line verdict

ODI is the "invest into a foreign entity" route, and it is a different lane from buying a GIFT City fund or a personal LRS remittance: it is triggered by unlisted equity, a listed stake of 10% or more, or any control stake (a listed position under 10% with no control is OPI, not ODI); the entity automatic limit is 400% of net worth (the 26 May 2026 amendment, up from 300%) with the USD 1B/FY financial commitment moving to a prior-approval route; the individual is equity-only and capped at LRS USD 250k/FY; and it is a regulated Form FC-ODI filing that precedes the remittance - skip it and the FEMA 3x / compounding layer is the price.

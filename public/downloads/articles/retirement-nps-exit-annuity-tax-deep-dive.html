Your NPS at 60: The 80/20 Exit, the 60%-Exemption Cap, and What the Annuity Leg Actually Pays

The retirement-exit layer over NPS: what the PFRDA 16-Dec-2025 amendment changed (80% lump / 20% annuity, deferment to 85, the Rs 8L small-corpus 100% rule), why the Section 10(10A) exemption is capped at 60% of contributions even when you take 80%, how to pick among the annuity types, and the new-regime 80CCD(2) interaction.

DhanPedia · Author Chirag Singhal · Verified 2026-10-18

The retirement & FIRE / annuities article built your corpus math and the decumulation blueprint. This article is the NPS exit layer — the moment where a 15-30 year investment decision meets three rules most investors have never read together: the PFRDA 16 December 2025 exit amendment, the Section 10(10A) exemption cap, and the annuity market. Get all three wrong and you either pay tax on money the law intends to be exempt, or you buy an annuity that undershoots your survivor income.

<2>What the PFRDA 16-Dec-2025 amendment changed

For non-government subscribers (the All-Citizen / Corporate model), the Exits & Withdrawals amendment that took effect 16 December 2025 reset the default exit split:

LegBefore Dec-2025From Dec-2025
Lump sum you may take at 60,Up to 60% of corpus,Up to 80% of corpusAnnuity you must buy,At least 40%,At least 20%Deferral of the annuity purchase,Up to age 75,Up to age 85Small-corpus full-withdrawal threshold,Rs 5L,Rs 8L — a corpus up to Rs 8L is a 100% lump-sum exit, no annuityPremature exit (permitted, before 60),20% lump / 80% annuity,20% lump / 80% annuity (unchanged)

The 85-year deferral is the sleeper feature: you can keep the annuity corpus compounding inside the scheme and start the pension later, which is a real response to the fact that Indian life expectancy is still climbing. The Rs 8L small-corpus rule is the one that matters for a short-tenure subscriber (e.g. NPS Vatsalya, or a 5-year stint) — under Rs 8L you simply walk away with everything.

<2>The exemption cap that surprises people: 10(10A) says 60%, not 80%

Here is the rule that quietly costs the most. The PFRDA amendment lets you take up to 80% of your corpus as a lump sum at 60 — but the Income-tax Act exemption on that lump sum is capped at 60% of your total contributions (self + employer), under Section 10(10A) / 10(i)(xii).

$$ \text{Exempt lump sum} \;=\; \min\big(\text{lump sum taken},\; 0.60 \times \text{total contributions}\big) $$

The two percentages describe different things:

NumberWhat it controls
80% (PFRDA),The maximum share of the corpus you are allowed to take out as cash60% (Income-tax),The maximum share of your contributions the tax system will not touch

Consequence: if your NPS corpus has appreciated well past 1.33 times your contributions (which is the exact point where the 80% of corpus equals 60% of contributions), the extra 20% of corpus you are allowed to take is taxable at your slab rate. That is the gap the NPS retirement-exit calculator prices for you — a 30-year, 20k/month NPS at 9% build-up has a corpus far above 1.33x contributions, so the "taxable lump-sum portion" is a real, non-trivial line item.

One honesty note: the 60%-cap wording in Section 10(10A) sits in the 1961 Act and the Act-2025 renumbering has to be checked for your exit year — a CA should confirm the exact current-year provision before you act, because this is where the Act-2025 transition (effective 1 April 2026) has produced genuine confusion.

<2>The annuity leg: which of the five PFRDA variants you actually want

The minimum 20% (now that it is 20%, not 40%) still has to buy a PFRDA-empanelled annuity from one of the licensed ASPs. The five standard variants and who each suits:

Annuity typeRate band (age 60)Best for
Annuity for Life,Highest (~6-7%),Single / no dependents, maximum monthly incomeJoint Life (spouse continues),~5-6%,Married, want the survivor coveredJoint Life with Return of Purchase Price,~4-5%,Married + want the balance returned to the nomineeAnnuity Certain (5/10/15/20 yrs),Lower,Short guaranteed period, then nominee incomeLife with Annual Increase (1-3%),Lower start, grows,Inflation worry, willing to take a low first payment

The rate band matters more than the marketing suggests: on a Rs 80L annuity corpus, the difference between a 7% and a 5% life annuity is Rs 1.6L a year of income — a ₹1.6L/yr gap that compounds for decades. Shop the empanelled ASP quotes; the PFRDA empanelment list (15 providers as of 2026) is the universe you compare across.

<2>The new-regime interaction: 80CCD(2) is still your friend

From the 80C-baskets article: 80CCD(2) — your employer's NPS contribution, up to 14% of Basic+DA in the new regime — is one of the only deductions that survives the new regime. The NPS-specific points that decide the NPS tax position:

  • 80CCD(1B) Rs 50,000 is old-regime-only (verified in Wave 8; a conflicting new-regime claim was

resolved to old-only). In the new regime your self NPS deduction does not exist beyond the standard deduction.

  • 80CCD(1) (your own Tier-1, within the 80C basket) is old-regime-only.
  • 80CCD(2) is both-regimes.
  • The corpus exemption cap (10(10A)) and the annuity-income slabs are the exit-year tax, separate

from the entry-year deductions.

So a new-regime NPS subscriber's tax story splits into two: entry is the employer 80CCD(2) deduction (worth enormously at 30% marginal), exit is the 60% exemption cap on the lump sum + slab tax on the annuity income. The entry deduction and the exit cap are two different levers and they do not net against each other.

The Senior-Citizen Regime Decision: When the Old Regime Wins for a Retiree (and the 87A Trap It Hides)

The terminal-rung tax layer: how a retiree living on interest and pension should choose between the old and new regimes in FY 2026-27 — the age-based old-regime exemptions (3L senior / 5L super-senior), the 80TTB interest deduction, the 80D enhanced cap, the ₹1L senior TDS threshold + Form 15H, the advance-tax exemption, and the counterintuitive case where the new regime's 87A-to-12L still wins.

DhanPedia · Author Chirag Singhal · Verified 2026-10-18

The 80C basket regime decision answered "which regime for a working earner." This article is the same question for the terminal rung of your ladder — the retiree living on pension, interest and a SCSS, with no salary. The answer is not the one most people assume: because the new regime's 87A rebate keeps income up to ₹12L tax-free while the old regime's 87A only reaches ₹5L, the new regime can still be cheaper for an interest-heavy retiree even after you give up 80TTB and the higher age exemption.

<2>The age brackets — and which regime they matter in

The Income-tax Act splits older taxpayers into two groups, and the distinction only carries weight under the old regime:

GroupAgeOld-regime basic exemptionNew regime
Regular taxpayer,Below 60,₹2.5L,No age benefitSenior citizen,60-79,₹3L,No age benefitSuper senior citizen,80+,₹5L,No age benefit

Under the new regime (the default), age does not matter: everyone gets the same slabs and the same ₹4L nil band plus the 87A rebate to ₹12L. So a super-senior who moves to the new regime silently gives up ₹1L of extra exemption (₹5L old vs ₹4L new) — that is the cost of the 87A sweetener.

<2>The two benefits the old regime gives a retiree (and both are old-only)
  1. Section 80TTB — the interest deduction. A senior citizen can deduct up to ₹50,000 (AY

2026-27; a Budget-2026 raise to ₹1L has been reported but is unverified — treat it as ₹50,000 until a CA confirms) of interest income from savings accounts, FDs, RDS, post-office deposits and SCSS. It is far more generous than 80TTA (₹10,000, savings-only, for non-seniors). It exists only in the old regime — opt into the new one and it vanishes.

  1. The higher age exemption above (₹3L/₹5L), which the new regime does not give.

The retirement instrument most retirees hold — SCSS at 8.2% on up to ₹30L — is exactly where the tax surprises them: the interest is fully taxable as Income from Other Sources at slab. The only two reliefs are both old-regime: the deposit counts for 80C (up to ₹1.5L in the year you invest) and up to ₹50,000 of the interest is sheltered by 80TTB.

$$ \text{SCSS after-tax income} \;\approx\; \text{interest} \times (1 - \text{slab rate}) \;-\; \text{the 80TTB shelter you actually get} $$

<2>The counterintuitive result: the new regime often wins anyway

This is the finding most "save with 80TTB" articles get wrong. Run a 66-year-old with ₹8.46L of income (₹2.46L SCSS interest + ₹6L pension):

RegimeWhat appliesTax
New,87A rebate to ₹12L → zero,₹0Old,80TTB (₹50k) + 80D + 3L exemption, but 87A only to ₹5L,₹59,200

Below ₹12L of income, the new regime's 87A rebate swallows the entire tax bill, so giving up 80TTB costs you nothing — the old regime's deductions have no base tax left to reduce. The old regime only starts to win above ₹12L, where its 80TTB + 80D + higher exemption can beat the new regime's steeper top bands. That boundary is why you must compute the decision rather than assume "80TTB means old."

Below that, no TDS.Form 15H (the senior version of 15G),If your total income is below the taxable limit even after crossing ₹1L, submit 15H to the bank to stop TDS at source — avoid the wait for a refund.Advance-tax exemption for retirees,A senior with no business income is exempt from advance tax; pay the whole bill as self-assessment tax by the filing due date without 234B/234C interest (the advance-tax article covers the clock).Section 194P convenience,A senior aged 75+ whose only income is pension + interest from the same bank can have that bank compute and deduct the correct tax — and not file an ITR at all.

The 194P route is the one most retirees never discover: a 78-year-old on ₹4L of pension + interest from one bank may have literally zero filing to do.

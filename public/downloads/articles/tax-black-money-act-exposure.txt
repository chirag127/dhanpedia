# Black Money Act Exposure: What Happens if You Miss FAST-DS *and* Schedule FA (FY 2026-27)

The counterfactual layer the FAST-DS article pointed to: the Black Money Act 2015 enforcement exposure for an undisclosed foreign asset that is neither regularised under FAST-DS 2026 nor disclosed in Schedule FA - the Section 40 30% tax + the Section 41 penalty of 3× the tax (= 90% of value, a 120% total) + the flat ₹10L-per-year Section 43/44 Schedule FA penalty (waived for non-immovable assets at or under the ₹20L Finance-Act-2026 threshold), and the three ways out (FAST-DS, Schedule FA going forward, or the open-ended BMA assessment). The 'do nothing' price is 120% + a recurring flat penalty; the defined cost is 60% or the flat fee.

The FAST-DS 2026 article (./tax-fastds-2026-declaration.mdx) is the defined-cost layer: pay 60% of the value (Category A) or a flat Rs. 1L (Category B) and get immunity. This page is the counterfactual layer the FAST-DS article deliberately left pointed at but did not compute: what happens to an undisclosed foreign asset if you do nothing — no FAST-DS declaration, no Schedule FA disclosure, no successor scheme. That "nothing" is not neutral; it is the Black Money (Undisclosed Foreign Income and Assets) and Imposition of Tax Act, 2015 enforcement track, and its price is structurally worse than any amnesty cost. The whole reason FAST-DS exists is that this counterfactual is the number you are trying to avoid. Everything below is a planning model; the actual BMA assessment is a departmental order, not a calculator output.

THE COUNTERFACTUAL EXPOSURE (THE "DO NOTHING" PRICE)
====================================================

The Black Money Act 2015 is the enforcement framework; FAST-DS is a one-time way out of it. For an undisclosed foreign asset / income that you have not regularised and not disclosed, the counterfactual exposure is built from three sections:

Section: **Section 40**,A tax on the undisclosed foreign income / asset,**30% of the value**  |  What it charges: **Section 41**,A penalty on *concealment* of the income in relation to a foreign asset,**3 × the tax = 90% of the value**  |  The math: **Section 43 / 44**,A flat penalty for failing to disclose the asset in **Schedule FA** (or furnishing inaccurate particulars),**₹10,00,000 per defaulting assessment year**

The first two are the headline number: 30% + 90% = 120% of the undisclosed value. That is deliberately more than the value itself — the Act is designed so that the cost of being caught exceeds the asset, which is the whole deterrent. The Section 43/44 flat penalty is then added on top, per year of non-disclosure, so a multi-year omission compounds a Rs. 10L charge on the back of the 120%.

Worked: a Rs. 50L foreign bank account that was never in any Schedule FA and is not FAST-DS-regularised, with the omission spanning 3 assessment years:

Component: Section 40 tax (30% × ₹50L),**₹15L**  |  Amount: Section 41 penalty (3 × tax = 90% × ₹50L),**₹45L**

Against that, the FAST-DS Category A defined cost for the same Rs. 50L is Rs. 30L (60% of value). The Rs. 60L gap is not an accident — it is the exact price of the one-time amnesty, and it is the number that answers "should I file FAST-DS before 31 December 2026?" for a category-A asset: yes, by a factor of 2–3, and the gap widens with every additional non-disclosure year (each adds another Rs. 10L to the counterfactual).

THE FINANCE ACT 2026 THRESHOLD EXCEPTION (THE ONE SOFT EDGE)
============================================================

There is exactly one place the counterfactual gets softer, and it was introduced by the Finance Act 2026: the flat Rs. 10L Section 43/44 penalty does not apply where the aggregate value of non-immovable foreign assets stays within Rs. 20 lakh. The logic is proportionality — a small, forgotten forex balance is not worth a Rs. 10L penalty stamp, so the flat penalty is waived for it.

Three precise consequences the exception does and does not change:
• It only removes the flat Rs. 10L penalty, not the 120%. Even a Rs. 15L non-immovable account that escapes the flat penalty still owes the Section 40 30% + Section 41 90% = 120% on the value if it is assessed as undisclosed. The soft edge is real but narrow.
• It is value- and asset-class-specific. The waiver is for non-immovable assets (a foreign bank account, foreign shares, a foreign fund) at or under Rs. 20L in aggregate. A foreign immovable property — no matter how small — never gets the waiver; the flat penalty applies to it in full.
• It is a 2026-budget position and can move. The Rs. 20L threshold is a Finance-Act 2026 figure; a later Budget can raise, lower or repeal it, so the "is my small account safe from the flat penalty?" answer should be re-checked each year (the update log carries this to the 2027-04-17 review).

Alt text: The Black Money Act counterfactual: a non-immovable foreign asset under Rs. 20L escapes only the flat Rs. 10L penalty (FA-2026) but still owes 120% (Section 40 + 41); anything over Rs. 20L or any foreign immovable property owes 120% plus the flat Rs. 10L/yr. Both are far above the FAST-DS 60% / flat-Rs. 1L defined-cost routes.

THE THREE WAYS OUT (AND WHY THEY ARE NOT THE SAME THING)
========================================================

There are three distinct routes, and conflating them is the core mistake:
1. FAST-DS 2026 (the one-time defined cost, Category A = 60%, Category B = flat Rs. 1L, deadline 31 Dec 2026). This is the only route that trades a calculable, lower cost for immunity from further tax, penalty and BMA prosecution. It is the route the counterfactual exists to make unnecessary. → the FAST-DS article (./tax-fastds-2026-declaration.mdx)
2. Schedule FA going forward (the routine, ongoing obligation). For every year after the asset is properly reported, the obligation is to keep disclosing it in Schedule FA each year — there is no penalty for correct disclosure, only for omission or inaccuracy. This is not an "out"; it is the normal state the counterfactual is a failure of.
3. The open-ended BMA assessment (the "do nothing" counterfactual — 120% + flat Rs. 10L/yr). This is what happens if both of the above are skipped: the department finds the undisclosed asset (often through the information exchange with the home-country tax authority), assesses Section 40 + 41, and stacks the Section 43/44 flat penalty for each defaulting year. There is no cap, no deadline and no amnesty on this route — it is the open-ended exposure the Act is built to impose.

The decision is therefore not "BMA vs FAST-DS" — it is: did I use route 1 (FAST-DS) while the window was open, and am I now on route 2 (Schedule FA)? If the answer to route 1 is "no" (the window closed, or the asset was above the FAST-DS ceiling), the only route left is 3, and the counterfactual in the first table is your liability.

THE PENALTY IS PER DEFAULTING YEAR (THE COMPOUNDING TRAP)
=========================================================

The Section 43/44 flat penalty is the part most people underestimate: it is Rs. 10L per assessment year the Schedule FA omission persisted, not a one-time charge. An asset that was undisclosed across 2022-23, 2023-24 and 2024-25 carries three flat penalties (Rs. 30L) on top of the 120%. This is why the "I'll just disclose it in this year's return" instinct is partly right and partly a trap: a late Schedule FA entry fixes the current-year omission but does not retroactively erase the flat penalties for the prior defaulting years — and a late self-disclosure, unlike a FAST-DS declaration, gives you no immunity from the Section 41 concealment penalty for the years you had already omitted. The FAST-DS route is the only one that bundles "I declare, I pay a defined amount, and I'm done" into a single instrument.

A WORKED EXAMPLE: THE RS. 50L FORGOTTEN ACCOUNT, THREE ROUTES
=============================================================

Same facts (a Rs. 50L non-immovable foreign account, never reported, 3 years of omission). The three routes price differently:

Route: **FAST-DS Category A** (if it was *never* taxed elsewhere),**₹30L** (60% of value),**Immunity** from further tax, penalty & BMA prosecution — the clean exit  |  Cost: **FAST-DS Category B** (if it *was* taxed elsewhere but missed Schedule FA),**₹1,00,000 flat**,Immunity for the omission — by far the cheapest if the asset was ever taxed  |  What you get: **Do nothing → BMA counterfactual**,**₹90L** (₹60L = 120% + ₹30L flat / 3 yr),No immunity — you have simply paid the open-ended assessment, and the Section 41 "may" reading (a Mumbai ITAT Special Bench held the penalty word "may" to be *directory*, not *mandatory*, in *Mr. Vinil Venugopal & Mrs. Ranjeeta Vinil*) only gives the department *discretion*, not you a right

The Rs. 90L vs Rs. 30L vs Rs. 1L spread on the identical asset is the entire argument for reading the FAST-DS article's "was it ever taxed?" question first: the reclassification from Category A to Category B alone saves Rs. 29L, and choosing any FAST-DS route over "do nothing" saves Rs. 60L+.

WHEN THE COUNTERFACTUAL IS SURVIVABLE (THE HONEST LAYER)
========================================================
• The Rs. 20L non-immovable FA-2026 soft edge is the one case where "do nothing" is less catastrophic than the 120%-plus-flat picture: a small forgotten forex account under Rs. 20L still owes 120% (not a tiny number, but bounded) and skips the flat-penalty stacking. It is the only scenario where the open-ended route is arguably survivable, and even there the 120% is 2× the FAST-DS Category A cost.
• The per-year flat penalty makes time the enemy. The longer the omission, the more Rs. 10L stacks; this is a deadline argument, not a strategy argument.
• The "may" discretion is not a defence you can rely on. The ITAT's directory reading of Section 43 means the department may or may not impose the flat penalty — that is a coin flip in the assessee's disfavour, not a planning input. You do not plan around the hope that an officer uses their discretion leniently.
• Above the FAST-DS ceilings (undisclosed > Rs. 1 cr, or missed-Schedule-FA > Rs. 5 cr), FAST-DS simply cannot be used, and the entire counterfactual is the only route — which is the honest limit of the amnesty and the reason a large undisclosed foreign asset needs professional handling, not a calculator.

WHAT TO DO (BEFORE THE WINDOW CLOSES AND BEYOND IT)
===================================================
1. Answer the Category question first (was this asset ever taxed or reported?) — that decides FAST-DS Category B (the flat-Rs. 1L cheap route) vs Category A (60%), and the "do nothing" counterfactual is the fallback for both if you miss the window.
2. Check the value as of 31 March 2026 and the Rs. 20L non-immovable FA-2026 threshold to know whether the flat-penalty soft edge applies to your specific asset.
3. Count the defaulting years — the flat penalty is per year, and this is the number that makes time the enemy.
4. Run the black-money-act-exposure calculator (/calculators/black-money-act-exposure) for the "do nothing" total, and the fast-ds-declaration calculator (/calculators/fast-ds-declaration) for the "defined cost" total; the gap between the two is your amnesty's value.
5. If the window is still open (before 31 Dec 2026) and the asset is within a FAST-DS ceiling, file FAST-DS — it is the only defined-cost, immunity-bearing route.
6. If the window has closed or the asset is above the ceilings, keep the asset on Schedule FA every going-forward year and get professional advice on the open-ended BMA exposure for the prior defaulting years.
7. Reconcile with a CA — the BMA "may" discretion, the FA-2026 threshold, and the per-year penalty stacking are all facts that a specific asset's record must be checked against, not assumed.

THE ONE-LINE VERDICT
====================

"Do nothing" on an undisclosed foreign asset is not a neutral position — it is the Black Money Act counterfactual: 120% of the value (Section 40 30% tax + Section 41 90% penalty) plus a flat Rs. 10L per defaulting year (Section 43/44), softened only for a non-immovable asset under the Rs. 20L Finance-Act-2026 threshold. The defined-cost routes that replace it are FAST-DS Category A (60% of value) or Category B (a flat Rs. 1L), both carrying immunity, and both only while the one-time window (31 Dec 2026) is open. The gap between "do nothing" (Rs. 90L on a Rs. 50L/3-yr asset) and the cheapest defined route (Rs. 1L) is not an artefact — it is the entire economic reason the amnesty was passed.

# Banking & Cash in India: Deposit Safety, FDs, Small-Finance Banks, and Where to Actually Keep Cash

DICGC's ₹5 lakh limit, bank categories, FD mechanics and current rates, small-finance and payment-bank differences, sweep-in, overnight funds, and where a ₹10 lakh emergency fund should live.

Scope note. This article covers the safety mechanics of Indian bank deposits (what is insured, what is not, what bank categories mean), FD economics at current rates, and where cash should live for a Rs. 10 lakh investor. It is not a bank-recommendation article. Figures marked "(verified 2026-10-16)" were checked against the cited sources; interest rates move quarterly.

WHY "BANK IS SAFE" IS ONLY HALF THE TRUTH
=========================================

India has a deposit-insurance floor, not a guarantee. The DICGC — a wholly-owned subsidiary of the RBI — insures deposits up to Rs. 5 lakh per depositor, per bank, per "same right and same capacity." [src-dicgc-deposit-insurance]

That means:
• Your Rs. 50 lakh in SBI + Rs. 50 lakh in HDFC + Rs. 50 lakh in ICICI is insured in full if each is in a separate bank and all in your individual (non-JOINT, non-HUF) name.
• Rs. 50 lakh split across five SBI accounts in your name (current + savings + FD1 + FD2 + salary) is insured only up to Rs. 5 lakh total — DICGC aggregates same depositor, same bank, same capacity.
• Joint accounts are capped at Rs. 5 lakh per joint holder, not per account. A 2-person joint FD of Rs. 100 lakh at one bank is insured only Rs. 10 lakh in total.
• SGBs, PPF, KVP, SCSS, NPS, bank NPS, and government-guaranteed retirement assets are NOT DICGC-covered — they are protected by the government or by scheme law, but they do not add to your Rs. 5L DICGC headroom.
• Sweep-ins and overnight funds (which park excess balance in overnight/liquid mutual funds or call accounts) lose DICGC cover the moment the funds flow out of the bank's own balance sheet. A sweep account is effectively an MF exposure; the insurance does not follow.

DICGC IS THE FLOOR, NOT THE CEILING
===================================

The Rs. 5L limit has not changed in decades. It is a last-resort safety net — in practice, when a bank goes under, the government (via RBI) has historically stepped in to protect depositors beyond DICGC (e.g., Yes Bank 2020: RBI extended its safety net to all depositors, not just up to Rs. 5L). But DICGC is the legal minimum, and yes-bank/IDBI-style bailouts are discretionary.

Rule of thumb: if you hold more than Rs. 5L at a single bank in your individual name in insured deposits, the excess is technically uninsured.

FD MECHANICS — WHAT A "1-YEAR FD AT 6.75%" ACTUALLY MEANS
=========================================================

A Fixed Deposit is a deposit account with a fixed interest rate and a fixed maturity. Interest can be paid:
• Credited at maturity (CUM) — the classic option; you get principal + total interest lump-sum at maturity.
• Periodic payouts (monthly/quarterly/semi-annual) — interest is taxed each year at your slab; the FD principal is not compounded.

Taxation of FD interest: taxed at your slab rate under "Income from Other Sources." TDS at 10% applies on interest >Rs. 40,000 per year for individuals (Rs. 50,000 for senior citizens). No deduction on interest earned; no 80C benefit on the principal.

Premature withdrawal: most banks charge a penalty of 0.50–1.00 percentage point on the rate; you may be locked for the penalty period. If you break a 3-year FD after 2 years, you lose the interest spread plus the penalty.

Rule of thumb: if you need the cash inside 1 year, an overnight/liquid fund is fine (0% LCG after 91 days) and more liquid than an FD. If you don't need it for 1–3 years, an FD at 7–8% (spread across banks for DICGC) typically beats overnight funds on after-tax yield.

WHERE SHOULD RS. 10 LAKH EMERGENCY + CASH RESERVE ACTUALLY LIVE?
================================================================

This is not an investment question — it is a liquidity and safety question. A typical Rs. 10L individual investor (no home loan) might structure:

Bucket: **Emergency fund (6 months' spending)**,₹2–3L,Overnight/liquid MF (Gilt/overnight, under ₹5L per fund),Instant T+1/T+2 access; no TDS; LCG zero after 91 days  |  Amount: **Opportunity/sinkhole reserve**,₹2–3L,3–6 month FDs across 2 major banks (each ≤₹5L DICGC),Locked-in spread over money-market; DICGC-protected  |  Where it lives: **Sweep / idle-cash auto-parking**,₹1–2L,Bank sweep-in auto (overnight) or liquid fund,Captures idle salary-account balances; *loses* DICGC cover once in the sweep MF — accept this; the amount is small  |  Rationale: **FD ladder**,₹3–4L,1/2/3-year FDs across SBI + 1 SFB + 1 PVT bank (each ≤₹5L DICGC),7–8% effective if staggered

Do NOT keep Rs. 10L in a single bank's savings account. The savings-account interest (~2–3%) is negligible; the FD rates (6–8.5%) are the real yield, and the DICGC limit applies to total per-bank per-depositor per-capacity deposits (including savings + FDs combined).

THE 7-DAY CREDIT-BUREAU REPORTING CHANGE (2026)
===============================================

RBI's 2025–26 Credit Information Reporting framework (amended July 2026) moved credit-bureau reporting from monthly → fortnightly → weekly in phases. [src-rbi-credit-reporting-2026]

What changed for you:
• Lenders must now upload EMI/overdue data to CIBIL/Experian/Equifax/CRIF on 4 fixed dates every month (9th, 16th, 23rd, last day) from July 2026 (framework start April 1, 2026; full weekly reporting from July 1, 2026).
• A single missed EMI now hits your credit score within days, not 30–60 days.
• A paid-off loan or a cleared default now recovers your score faster too.

Practical implication: if you take a personal loan, credit-card EMI, or home loan, the credit-bureau "window" for repairing damage after a default is now measured in weeks, not months. This makes consistent, on-time EMIs more valuable than it was in 2024. See the Credit & Loans article (/articles/pf-credit-loans-debt) for the full prepay-vs-invest framework.

SWEEP-INS: THE QUIET YIELD-LOSS
===============================

Most salary accounts have a sweep-in facility: when your balance exceeds Rs. 1L–Rs. 5L, the excess is automatically moved into an overnight mutual fund or call-money account (typically 5.5–6.0% effective). This is not a bank FD and not DICGC-insured.

The yield trap: if you hold Rs. 3L permanently swept, you are earning ~5.8% in a liquid fund rather than ~7.1% in a bank FD or ~8.15% in PPF. The sweep is a convenience default, not a choice. If you know you will not need that Rs. 3L for 3 months+, move it to a proper FD ladder or PPF contribution.

PAYMENT BANKS: WHAT YOU CAN AND CAN'T DO
========================================

Payment banks (Airtel Pay, Paytm Payments Bank, NPS India Pay) are deposit-taking but non-lending — they cannot give out loans (except tiny, cap-managed credit against deposits). They are DICGC-insured up to Rs. 5L.

Limits:
• Maximum deposit per customer: Rs. 2 lakh (total across all payment-bank accounts).
• No FDs; savings interest only (~3–4%).
• Good for UPI/mobile wallets; do not store emergency funds here — the Rs. 2L cap alone makes it unsuitable.

SMALL-FINANCE BANK FDS: THE HIGH-YIELD CORNER
=============================================

SFBs (Ujjivan, Jan SFB, AU SFB, Fincap, ESAF, etc.) have been advertising senior-citizen FDs up to 8.5% (verified 2026-10-16). [src-fd-rates-aug2026]

The trade-off:
• Return: ~1.5–2.0 percentage points above major private banks.
• Risk: the bank's capital base is smaller; RBI imposes a Rs. 25L per-depositor cap on SFB deposits (total across all products). Your DICGC insurance still stops at Rs. 5L.
• Liquidity: SFBs have far fewer branches; most digital onboarding works, but physical paperwork at maturity can be slower.

For a Rs. 10L investor: use 1 SFB FD slot of ≤Rs. 5L (to stay in DICGC) at 8.0–8.5%, combined with major-bank FDs for the rest. Do not concentrate more than one SFB slot.

WHERE DICGC DOES NOT APPLY
==========================

These common "safe-sounding" instruments are not DICGC-insured:

Instrument: **SGB (existing, traded on NSE/BSE)**,A bond, not a bank deposit  |  Why it's not DICGC: **PPF**,Govt scheme — sovereign-guaranteed, but not bank-deposit insurance

Critical distinction: an NBFC FD (e.g., a 9.5% "corporate FD" from a Finance company) is not a bank FD. It is a debenture with the NBFC's credit risk. If the NBFC defaults, you are an unsecured creditor — not a DICGC-protected depositor. See the Fixed Income & Credit article (/articles/fixed-income-bonds-credit) for how to underwrite an NBFC debenture.

THE "WHAT IF THE BANK FAILS" CHECKLIST
======================================

If you have more than Rs. 5L total at one bank in your individual name:
1. Total up: savings + current + all FDs + recurring deposits + loan account balances (loans offset against your DICGC headroom) + any joint accounts where you are a co-holder.
2. Compare to Rs. 5L: if you exceed it, move the excess to a second bank or to a PPF/government scheme (not another bank at the same).
3. Check "same capacity": a Rs. 3L HUF FD and a Rs. 3L individual FD at the same bank count as separate DICGC heads (HUF vs. individual = different "capacity").
4. Joint accounts: each joint holder gets up to Rs. 5L separately. A 2-person joint FD of Rs. 10L at one bank is insured Rs. 5L per person = Rs. 10L total — you are safe.
5. Sweep-in: if you have a large sweep-in balance, know that the swept portion has left the bank's balance sheet and is not DICGC-insured. It is in an MF.

Bottom line: the DICGC limit is Rs. 5L per person per bank per capacity. Build your bank portfolio (and your FD portfolio) around that line, not in hope that a bailout will cover the excess.
